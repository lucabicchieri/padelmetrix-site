Padel court surfaces: types of artificial grass and how they play

Published on 18 November 2026 · by Luca Bicchieri, PadelMetrix

Almost all padel courts are sand-filled artificial grass, but the grass is not all the same: fibrillated, monofilament and texturised fibres change ball speed, grip and durability. The rules also allow porous concrete and synthetic surfaces, as long as the bounce is consistent and water doesn't pool.

What the rules say

  • Allowed materials: porous concrete, concrete, synthetic and artificial grass (FIP, regolamento 2021).
  • Colours: preferably green, light blue or terracotta, uniform; black is allowed indoors only.
  • Flatness: average level differences under 3 mm over 3 m.
  • Lines: 5 cm wide, preferably white or black.

The three types of artificial grass

FibreWhat it isPlayDurability and care
FibrillatedWide fibres split at the tip that curl and hold the sandFaster, more consistent bounceShorter life
MonofilamentSingle, stiffer fibres that stay uprightSlows the ball slightly: slower playLasts longer, needs frequent brushing; common at elite level
TexturisedCurled fibres, often combined with monofilamentsGood balance of grip and slideRecommended for professional courts

Sources: Consumoteca; İlke Sport.

In every case the surface needs short pile and dense stitching (İlke Sport). The quartz sand between the fibres provides grip and cushions footsteps; that's why the fibres must be brushed to keep them upright and redistribute the sand (Consumoteca).

The pros' court

Mondo was the official supplier of the World Padel Tour from 2015 to 2023 and since 2024 has supplied the turf for Premier Padel and the FIP. Its Premier Supercourt X3 combines monofilaments of different shapes with texturised fibres and uses silica sand infill (Mondo). It's the turf on the official Premier Padel courts, with their black and gold edition (Padel Addict).

New or worn grass: what changes

  • New grass full of sand: the ball slows down and bounces less, shoes grip more.
  • Worn grass with little sand: the ball is faster and lower, and you slide more. Be careful moving sideways.
  • Moisture: wet sand compacts the surface and changes the bounce; early in the morning or after rain the court plays differently.

Indoor or outdoor

The rules require at least 6 metres of clear height above the court (8 recommended for new facilities) and minimum lighting of 300 lux indoors and 200 lux outdoors for recreational play, 750 and 500 lux for national and international competitions (FIP, regolamento 2021). Indoors the ball is less affected by wind and moisture, so the bounce is more consistent.

The right shoes

On sand-filled artificial grass a herringbone sole works best; on indoor courts or courts with little sand an omni sole is fine. It's all in the padel shoe guide. For the other half of the court, read the article on glass and panoramic courts.

Sources: FIP, regolamento 2021; Consumoteca; İlke Sport; Mondo; Padel Addict.

Frequently asked questions

What surface is padel played on?

Almost always on sand-filled artificial grass; the rules also allow porous concrete and synthetic surfaces.

What's the difference between fibrillated and monofilament grass?

Fibrillated gives a faster, more consistent bounce but wears sooner; monofilament slows the ball slightly, lasts longer and needs frequent brushing.

What turf does Premier Padel use?

Mondo's Premier Supercourt X3, the official supplier since 2024: monofilaments of different shapes and texturised fibres with silica sand.
