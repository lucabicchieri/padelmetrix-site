Italy's young padel talents: who they are and who to watch (2026)

Published on 17 November 2026 · by Luca Bicchieri, PadelMetrix

Italian padel has a new generation that is already climbing high. Giulia Dal Pozzo, 21, is Italy's top player in the FIP world ranking; Michele Brambilla, born in 2005, is world university champion and has earned his first national team call-up; and the youth teams made the podium twice at the European Championships in Porto.

Giulia Dal Pozzo, the furthest ahead

Born in 2005 in Borgo Maggiore (San Marino) and raised between San Marino and Rimini, she is the daughter of former tennis player Francesca Guardigli (BNL Italy Major, Dal Pozzo). With the junior national team she won bronze at the 2023 Asunción World Championships; in December 2024, at the Milano Premier Padel P1, she came through qualifying with Caterina Baldi and reached the round of 16, beating Carolina Navarro. In 2025 she broke into the world top 100.

Today she is Italy's number 1 and around 23rd in the FIP ranking; in 2026 she plays with Spain's Nuria Rodríguez (StudyPadel, Dal Pozzo). At the Doha World Championships in November she and Emily Stellato won the deciding match for bronze against France, 7-6 6-3 (FITP, bronzo mondiale 2026).

Michele Brambilla and Noa Bonnefoy, world university champions

In July 2026 Malaga hosted the first world university padel championship: the men's title went to Michele Brambilla and Noa Bonnefoy, both born in 2005, who beat another Italian pair, Simone Iacovino and Matteo Brambilla, 4-6 6-3 6-4 in the final (FIP, Mondiali universitari Malaga). Michele Brambilla then received his first senior call-up for the Doha World Championships (Agimeg, convocati Mondiali 2026).

The new Italian women: Caterina Baldi and Clarissa Aima

Caterina Baldi and Clarissa Aima also made their senior debut in Doha after a season of progress (Agimeg, convocati Mondiali 2026). Baldi had already jumped to 90th in the world at the end of 2025 with one title and two finals in FIP Bronze events (Padel Magazine, classifiche italiani); at the World Championships she and Giulia Sussarello won the first point of the bronze match, 4-6 6-1 6-3 (FITP, bronzo mondiale 2026). The Italian women finished third, their fourth world podium in the last four editions; the men came fourth.

Matteo Platania, from Sicily towards Premier Padel

Born in 2006, he grew up in Agrigento at his father's club and moved to Rome. In February 2025 he came through qualifying at the Gijón Premier Padel P2, winning three rounds without dropping a set. His stated goal: reach the world top 200 and then play regularly on the Premier Padel tour (SuperTennis, Platania).

Youth teams: a double podium at the Europeans

At the FIP Junior Euro Padel Cup in Porto in July 2026, Italy won silver with the girls and bronze with the boys (Sanremonews). The driving force of the boys' team was Pietro Giovannini, an Under 18 player from Imperia, at his last junior European Championship.

  • Under 18: Pietro Giovannini, Stefano Indomenico, Edoardo Riso, Gabriele Laurino; Vittoria Giraldi, Matilde Minelli, Matilde Laura, Lucrezia Piernera.
  • Under 16: Jacopo Battilani, Tommaso Sarti, Mark Giulio Menotti, Tommaso Rufo; Emma Speziali, Alessia Sgattoni, Flavia Maltese, Sophie Caruso.
  • Under 14: Giovanni Miriello, Lorenzo Margani, Gianmarco Tempreini, Francesco Filetti; Vittoria Conti, Matilde Scarcella, Marta La Carpia, Miriam Pentrella.

How to follow them

Young Italians mostly appear in FIP Promises events (Under 18) and in FIP Bronze and Silver tournaments, where they earn their first world ranking points; the best then enter qualifying at Premier Padel events such as the Rome Major at the Foro Italico. Frequently asked questions

Who are Italy's young padel talents?

The furthest ahead are Giulia Dal Pozzo (2005), Italy's top player in the FIP ranking, Michele Brambilla and Noa Bonnefoy (2005), world university champions, Caterina Baldi and Clarissa Aima, national team debutantes at the 2026 World Championships, and Matteo Platania (2006). Among juniors, Pietro Giovannini stands out.

Who is Giulia Dal Pozzo?

She is an Italian player born in 2005 in Borgo Maggiore (San Marino), daughter of former tennis player Francesca Guardigli. She is Italy's number 1, around 23rd in the FIP ranking, and won bronze at the 2026 World Championships with Italy.

How did Italy do at the 2026 Padel World Championships?

In Doha the women's team won bronze, beating France 2-0, their fourth podium in the last four editions; the men finished fourth after losing 2-1 to Portugal.
