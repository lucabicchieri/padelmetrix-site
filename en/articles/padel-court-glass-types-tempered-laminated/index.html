Padel court glass: tempered, laminated and panoramic courts

Published on 16 November 2026 · by Luca Bicchieri, PadelMetrix

Padel courts use three types of glass: 10 mm tempered, 12 mm tempered and 6+6 mm laminated. The rules allow all three. 12 mm is the standard for quality courts and tournaments; 10 mm is the cheapest and least safe; laminated is the most expensive and holds even when one pane breaks.

What the rules say

The FIP allows walls of any material, transparent or opaque, as long as the bounce is consistent. In the European Union glass must comply with EN 12150-1; the federation note allows monolithic tempered glass of at least 10 mm or 6+6 mm laminated glass (FIP, regolamento 2021). Back walls are 4 metres high: 3 of glass or wall and 1 of wire mesh.

There is also an alternative to glass: acrylic panels such as NXPadel's ComfortGlass, which the maker says weigh less than half as much, are nearly indestructible and bounce the same. These are the manufacturer's claims, not independently verified (Padel Addict).

Glass or wall?

Older or cheaper courts have brick or concrete walls. The rules require a single colour, preferably green, blue or terracotta, clearly distinct from the floor (FIP, regolamento 2021). Glass became the standard because spectators can see the play and the bounce is more even; old walls can have spots where the ball bounces less.

The official Premier Padel court is a super panoramic court with 12 mm tempered glass, FIP-approved, which can be fixed or portable and withstands winds of up to 120 km/h (Padel Addict).

Side walls: four variants

The rules allow four side configurations (FIP, regolamento 2021):

  • Two "stepped" ones: a 3 m wall for the first 2 m, then 2 m high, with mesh above.
  • Two "glass only" ones: 3 m glass for 4 m at each end, with no step, and mesh for the rest of the side.

Players feel the difference in the corners: with a step, the ball can change direction off the edge.

What it means for players

  • Bounce: glass is more consistent than walls, so playing the ball off the wall is more predictable.
  • Visibility: on panoramic courts you see balls leaving the court better and play with more visual space.
  • Safety: don't throw your racket at the glass, and report chips or loose panes to the club.

Frequently asked questions

How thick is padel court glass?

At least 10 mm if tempered; the standard for quality courts is 12 mm. Laminated 6+6 mm is the alternative.

Which glass is the safest?

Laminated 6+6 mm: if the inner pane breaks the outer one holds. Among tempered glass, 12 mm is safer than 10 mm.

What's the difference between a panoramic and a standard court?

Panoramic courts have hidden posts and full-height 12 mm glass on the sides and back; standard courts have glass only at the back and mesh on the sides.
